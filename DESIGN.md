# DESIGN.md

Visual direction and design principles for **karlthoren.de**.

## Design Concept

The current working design concept is:

# The Arcane Study

> Fantasy nerdery through the lens of a gentleman scholar.

The visual identity should feel like the personal study of someone who might spend the evening reading software documentation, writing fantasy fiction, examining a peculiar antique map and wondering whether the dragon drawn in its margin was intended as decoration or field documentation.

It combines:

- fantasy
- scholarship
- craftsmanship
- curiosity
- literature
- exploration
- quiet eccentricity

The result should feel mature without becoming solemn, and whimsical without becoming childish.

## Desired Impression

The site should feel:

- distinctive
- mature
- intelligent
- warm
- literary
- curious
- handcrafted
- slightly mysterious
- subtly eccentric
- contemporary

It should not feel like a fantasy-themed website template.

The fantasy influence should emerge through atmosphere, illustration, typography and detail rather than through constant explicit references.

## Core Visual Metaphor

Imagine a private study containing:

- dark wood
- worn leather
- bronze instruments
- old books
- warm lamplight
- maps
- curious specimens
- sketches
- notebooks
- strange objects collected over time

Now reinterpret that atmosphere as a modern website rather than reproducing the room literally.

The website should feel inspired by those materials and associations, not decorated like a historical film set.

## Color Direction

The preferred foundation is dark and warm.

Suitable background directions include:

- near-black green
- ink
- charcoal
- dark olive
- deep brown-green
- very dark aubergine

Avoid cold neutral black where a subtle warm or organic tint would provide more character.

Primary text should generally use warm light tones such as:

- ivory
- bone
- parchment-white
- warm grey

These should look refined rather than yellowed or artificially antique.

### Metallic Accent Family

The main accent language should come from metals and aged materials:

- muted gold
- antique gold
- brass
- bronze
- copper

These colors should usually be somewhat restrained rather than bright metallic yellow.

Possible secondary accent directions include:

- verdigris
- moss
- oxblood
- dark wine
- muted forest green
- desaturated teal

Color should support the atmosphere rather than dominate it.

## Color Principle

Prefer a small coherent palette.

A typical composition might consist of:

- one dark foundation
- one warm light text color
- one metallic primary accent
- one restrained secondary accent

Avoid rainbow gradients and large collections of unrelated accent colors.

## Typography

Typography is one of the most important parts of the identity.

The design should combine literary character with contemporary clarity.

A serif typeface is appropriate for:

- major headings
- quotations
- editorial statements
- selected labels

A clean sans-serif may be used for:

- navigation
- utility text
- technical information
- longer interface-oriented elements

The contrast between serif and sans-serif can represent the two sides of the site:

> imagination and structure  
> literature and technology  
> fantasy and research

Typography should feel confident and spacious.

Avoid ornamental faux-medieval fonts.

Fantasy atmosphere should not depend on Gothic or pseudo-runic typography.

## Layout

The layout should feel editorial rather than dashboard-like.

Prefer:

- strong typographic hierarchy
- generous negative space
- asymmetry
- occasional unexpected alignment
- fine rules and separators
- deliberate rhythm
- large focal elements
- restrained content density

Not every piece of content needs a container.

Not every topic needs a card.

Large areas of open space are allowed to be part of the composition.

## Geometry

The design may mix precise structure with subtle irregularity.

Useful elements include:

- fine lines
- circles
- diagrams
- cropped geometric forms
- frames
- registration marks
- small symbols
- unusual corner treatments

Rounded rectangles should be used deliberately rather than becoming the universal shape language.

## Materials

The design may visually reference materials such as:

- leather
- bronze
- brass
- dark wood
- ink
- paper
- oxidised copper
- clothbound books

These references should mostly influence:

- color
- texture
- line
- illustration
- typography

Avoid literal photorealistic material simulation unless there is a strong reason.

The goal is atmosphere, not skeuomorphism.

## Fantasy

Fantasy is explicitly welcome.

The design does not need to hide its nerdier side.

Possible motifs include:

- dragons
- monsters
- strange creatures
- swords
- towers
- magical artifacts
- mysterious plants
- fictional maps
- constellations
- heraldic shapes
- impossible architecture
- alchemical or invented instruments

However, these should appear selectively.

A single tiny dragon drawn into the margin may contribute more personality than an enormous photorealistic dragon covering the hero section.

Fantasy elements should feel discovered rather than advertised.

## Illustration Style

The design should leave room for whimsical minimalist illustrations.

Preferred directions include:

- ink drawing
- engraving-inspired line art
- marginalia
- bookplate illustration
- field-guide drawing
- natural-history sketch
- restrained silhouettes
- simplified technical diagrams

Illustrations may be elegant, strange, playful or mildly absurd.

A creature wearing spectacles, a dragon curled around a stack of books, or an obviously unnecessary diagram of a fictional artifact could all fit the visual language if executed with restraint.

The site is allowed to have a sense of humour.

## The Scholar

The scholarly side should not mean generic academia.

It is closer to:

- independent researcher
- obsessive collector
- gentleman naturalist
- writer with too many notebooks
- engineer examining an ancient device
- curious person following a question much further than strictly necessary

Visual references may therefore include:

- annotations
- catalogue numbers
- specimen labels
- footnotes
- diagrams
- coordinates
- handwritten-style marginal notes
- classification systems
- diagrams of questionable usefulness

These elements should be used sparingly enough to remain charming.

## Technology

Technology should fit naturally into the same world.

Avoid visually separating the site into:

> fantasy section = old books  
> technology section = blue glowing cyber interface

Instead, technology should feel like another subject being investigated in the same study.

Software architecture, APIs or programming can be presented with the same visual curiosity as fictional creatures or maps.

The underlying theme is not historical fantasy.

It is:

> curiosity about systems, real or imagined.

## Motion

Animation should be subtle and intentional.

Suitable motion includes:

- gentle reveal effects
- slowly moving decorative diagrams
- subtle line drawing
- understated hover transitions
- small illustration reactions
- gradual changes in texture or illumination

Avoid excessive:

- bouncing
- glowing
- parallax
- spinning
- constant ambient animation

Motion should reward attention rather than demand it.

## Texture

Subtle texture is encouraged.

Possible treatments include:

- film grain
- paper grain
- ink irregularity
- extremely faint scratches
- slightly imperfect lines

Texture should remain almost subconscious.

If the texture becomes one of the first things noticed, it is probably too strong.

## Navigation and Interface

Navigation should feel clean and modern.

Do not sacrifice usability for atmosphere.

Interactive elements should remain obvious enough to understand without explanation.

Styling may reference labels, tabs, bookmarks, catalogues or bookplates, but the interaction model should remain familiar.

## Whimsy

Whimsy is an important part of the identity.

The site should have occasional details that exist simply because they are delightful.

Examples:

- a tiny illustrated creature hidden near a section heading
- an absurd specimen number
- a diagram that labels something unnecessarily
- an unexpected marginal note
- a fictional cataloguing system
- a dragon used as a divider
- a microscopic sword icon where no sword icon is required

These details should be uncommon enough that discovering one feels rewarding.

## Avoid

Avoid drifting toward:

- generic SaaS design
- corporate blue-grey
- excessive glassmorphism
- generic cyberpunk
- neon overload
- constant gradients
- dashboard aesthetics
- medieval tavern aesthetics
- parchment everywhere
- faux runes
- Gothic-font overload
- steampunk cosplay
- photorealistic fantasy art as default decoration
- excessive ornamentation
- excessive cards
- overly cute fantasy imagery
- artificial "luxury" clichés

The site should not look like:

- a game launcher
- a fantasy wiki
- a consulting company
- a crypto project
- a medieval restaurant menu

## Balance

The identity depends on balancing several tensions:

```text
Fantasy          ↔ Restraint
Whimsy           ↔ Maturity
History          ↔ Modernity
Literature       ↔ Technology
Ornament         ↔ Negative space
Mystery          ↔ Usability
Scholarship      ↔ Playfulness
```

Neither side should completely dominate.

## Design Test

When considering a new visual element, ask:

> Could this plausibly belong in the digital study of a modern gentleman scholar who happens to be deeply interested in fantasy, technology and strange ideas?

If yes, it probably fits.

If it looks primarily like a current web-design trend with the project's colors applied on top, it probably needs more character.

## Final Principle

The site should feel less like a designed product and more like a place that gradually accumulated personality.

It should be polished enough to feel intentional, but strange enough to feel personal.
